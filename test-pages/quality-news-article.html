OpenAI Releases GPT-5 with Breakthrough Reasoning Capabilities

By Sarah Chen, Technology Correspondent
San Francisco, March 15, 2026

OpenAI unveiled GPT-5 on Monday, claiming the model can solve complex mathematical proofs and write production-ready code with 94% accuracy—a 23-percentage-point improvement over GPT-4.

The company demonstrated the system solving a previously unsolved number theory problem from the International Mathematical Olympiad in 47 seconds. "This represents a fundamental shift in AI capabilities," said OpenAI CEO Sam Altman at the launch event in San Francisco.

GPT-5 was trained on 15 trillion parameters using a new architecture called "Recursive Attention Networks" (RAN). The model consumed 50 exaflops of compute over six months, costing an estimated $800 million—five times the budget for GPT-4.

Early testing by independent researchers at MIT found GPT-5 scored 89% on the MMLU benchmark, compared to GPT-4's 86%. On coding tasks from HumanEval, it achieved 94.2% pass@1, surpassing GPT-4's 67%.

"The jump in reasoning ability is real," said Dr. Emily Rodriguez, an AI researcher at Stanford who tested the model. "It can now follow multi-step logical chains without hallucinating intermediate steps."

However, critics warn about safety implications. The Center for AI Safety released a statement expressing concern that GPT-5's capabilities could be misused for generating sophisticated phishing attacks or manipulating financial markets.

OpenAI addressed these concerns by implementing a new "Constitutional AI" layer that rejects 99.7% of harmful requests in internal testing. The company also limited API access to vetted enterprise customers for the first 90 days.

Pricing starts at $0.15 per 1,000 tokens for input and $0.60 per 1,000 tokens for output—triple GPT-4's rates. OpenAI justified the increase by citing the model's superior accuracy and reduced need for prompt engineering.

Microsoft, OpenAI's primary investor, announced it will integrate GPT-5 into GitHub Copilot by June 2026. The company expects the upgrade to reduce debugging time by 40% based on internal trials with 5,000 developers.

Competitors responded swiftly. Google's DeepMind announced it will release Gemini 2.0 "within weeks," while Anthropic said its Claude 4 model, scheduled for May, will match or exceed GPT-5's capabilities.

The release reignites debates about AI regulation. Senator Maria Gonzalez (D-CA) called for immediate hearings: "We cannot allow private companies to deploy systems this powerful without oversight."

OpenAI's Altman countered that overregulation could cede AI leadership to China, where companies face fewer restrictions. "The question isn't whether to build these systems—it's who builds them and under what values," he said.

GPT-5 will be available through OpenAI's API starting March 20, with ChatGPT Plus subscribers gaining access on April 1. The company plans to release a technical paper detailing the model's architecture in May.
